Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, **"Comprehensive Evaluation of Large
Language Models on Software Engineering Tasks: A Multi-Task Benchmark,"** for
consideration as a research article in *Automated Software Engineering*.

As large language models become embedded in development workflows,
practitioners must choose among many models with little evidence beyond
accuracy leaderboards. Our study addresses this gap by evaluating 11
state-of-the-art LLMs across five representative software engineering tasks
(bug fixing, feature development, refactoring, technical writing, and research
synthesis) under a uniform, automated verification harness, measuring not only
output quality but also completion efficiency.

The work makes the following contributions, which we believe align closely
with the journal's scope on generative-AI and LLM-enabled approaches to
automated software engineering:

1. An **efficiency-first** evaluation showing that models achieving identical
   perfect scores differ by **22×–53×** in completion time, tool usage, and
   estimated cost.
2. Evidence that **tool-usage frequency does not linearly correlate with
   success** (Pearson r = 0.077; 95% CI [−0.20, 0.34]), with one model using
   917 tool calls where another succeeded with 3.
3. A **taxonomy of agent inefficiency** (loop vs. inference inefficiency) and a
   constraint-driven **model-selection framework** for practitioners.
4. A fully **reproducible replication package** (data, verification scripts,
   and analysis code) released publicly.
